# Replay-table specification (real-LLM stage, Phases P3–P4)

Status: **built and replayed (2026-11-14), with the deviations listed in section 11.** The table is in `data/replay/` (built by `experiments/build_replay_table.py`), every policy is replayed by `experiments/run_replay.py`, and the results are in `docs/07_results_report.md` section 6. An earlier uncommitted attempt (205 rows) is superseded: its agent never saw a skill, its proposer read a placeholder instead of real failures, a proposer row was counted as a task success, and its token counts were estimates.

## 1. Idea
Run every candidate skill on every task of its family **once** with a real open model, store the outcomes and token counts in a table, then let every policy **replay** from that table. This gives real-LLM results, identical randomness for all policies, zero extra API cost per policy, and a ground-truth value for every skill (its success rate over all tasks), so regret against an oracle is measurable on real outputs.

**Why replay is valid:** the agent model is frozen and a skill is just text in its prompt, so the outcome of (skill, task) does not depend on earlier tasks. Anything that breaks this (stateful tools, history-dependent prompts) is out of scope for this stage.

## 2. Models (from the account's model list; chat availability and quality still to be confirmed by a small pilot)
| Role | Candidates seen in the list | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Agent (solves tasks, same for all policies) | `google/gemma-3-4b-it`, `mistralai/mistral-7b-instruct-v0.3`, `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-bolt-e4b`, `google/gemma-3-12b-it` | Small models leave headroom for skills to matter |
| Proposer (writes candidate skills from failures) | `deepseek-ai/deepseek-v4.1-flash`, `nvidia/llama-3.1-nemotron-70b-instruct`, `openai/gpt-oss-20b` | Stronger than the agent, still cheap |
| Judge (EvoGuard-style baseline, later) | a mid-size model different from the agent | Avoids self-agreement |
Selection rule: pick the agent whose empty-skill success rate lands in 25–60% across families in the pilot. Model identity and decoding settings are frozen in a committed config.

## 3. Task suite
- **5 families × 40 tasks**, single-turn, each with a **programmatic verifier** (exact-match or small unit test). No LLM grading.
- Each family has a **hidden convention** that is not stated in the task prompt but is deterministic and learnable from a failed attempt's expected-vs-actual diff. Candidate conventions (to be finalized and checked by a pilot):
  1. house date format and timezone rule,
  2. ticket-ID formatting with a checksum digit,
  3. unit conversion with a specific rounding convention,
  4. JSON field-naming and ordering convention,
  5. redaction rule for a defined set of fields.
- **Splits per family:** 30 stream tasks (the policy sees outcomes live) and 10 held-out tasks (used only for verification and for truth). A candidate written from one failure may overfit its triggering task, so held-out and unseen outcomes can differ from live ones. That is the generalization gap, now measured instead of assumed.
- **True value of a skill** = success rate over all 40 tasks of its family (stream plus held-out). Recorded in the table, hidden from policies.

## 4. Skill tree (so replay always has a real candidate to offer)
- Per family, a tree with **branching 3, depth 3**: 1 + 3 + 9 + 27 = 40 skills (the root is the empty skill).
- A child is generated by the proposer from the parent's failed tasks; its text and diff against the parent are stored.
- At replay, a policy at node X is offered X's children one at a time, in a fixed order. Accepting a child moves the incumbent to that child; rejecting it offers the next sibling. When children run out, no further candidates exist at that node (a finite-pool limitation that is stated in the write-up).
- Total skills ≈ 5 × 40 = 200.

## 5. Table contents
For every (skill, task) pair in the same family: `success` (0/1), `prompt_tokens`, `completion_tokens`, `model_id`, `decode_settings`, `response_hash`, `skill_hash`, `call_id`. For every skill: `skill_id`, `family`, `parent_id`, `text`, `diff`, `proposer_call_id`, `source_task_ids`, `true_value`, `parent_true_value`.

## 6. Size and rate cap (my choice; adjustable)
- Agent calls: 200 skills × 40 tasks = **8,000**.
- Proposer calls: about 200, plus pilot ≈ 150 calls. Total about **8,400 calls**.
- **Rate cap:** concurrency 4, at most 30 requests per minute overall, exponential backoff on 429/5xx, stop after 3 consecutive failures, hard cap of 12,000 calls and 20M tokens for the whole stage. At 30 requests per minute, 8,400 calls take roughly 4.7 hours of wall-clock; this is an estimate and the real limit may allow more.
- **Caching:** every call keyed by hash of (model, prompt, decode settings); reruns never repeat a paid call; runs are resumable.
- The account is described as free; I have not verified any limit or terms. I will read response headers and errors on the pilot and lower the cap if needed.

## 7. Pilot (before the full build)
- About 150 calls: 5 families × 6 tasks × empty skill, plus a handful of proposer calls and a determinism check (the same prompt twice at temperature 0).
- Pass criteria: agent empty-skill success 25–60% on at least 4 of 5 families; proposer outputs are valid skills; at least some candidates beat their parent and some do not; determinism holds on at least 95% of repeated calls. If a criterion fails, change the tasks or the model and repeat the pilot; do not proceed to the full table.

## 8. What the table lets us test about the simulator
- Compare the real distribution of Δ = child − parent true value with the EvoGuard-calibrated mixture in the simulator spec.
- Compare real verification-vs-truth gaps with σ_shift.
- If they differ, the simulator parameters are re-fit from the table, and the report states both.

## 9. Pre-registered checks
1. Policies never read `true_value` (same information-hiding test as the simulator).
2. Replay of a policy reproduces the same event log on every run.
3. Table completeness: every (skill, task) cell filled or flagged failed-call (failed calls are retried, never silently dropped).
4. Cost in the replay equals the logged tokens from the table, not the simulator's abstract units; both unit systems are reported.

## 10. Limitations
- Synthetic task families, not a standard benchmark. It is real LLM behavior on controlled tasks, which is what makes the explore/exploit mechanics visible; an ALFWorld-style multi-step stage is an optional later extension.
- Finite skill tree; one agent model; temperature-0 single draw per cell (determinism check only on a subsample).

## 11. As built (2026-11-14): deviations from this spec

| Spec | As built | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Small open models on an NVIDIA key | Google Gemini on the free tier: agent and proposer `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, judge `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, temperature 0 | The NVIDIA endpoint timed out; the Gemini key worked. The agent rewriting its own notes (same model as proposer) is the standard self-improvement setup. `gemini-3.5-flash` (a stronger proposer) allows 20 requests/day on the free tier |
| One agent call per (skill, task) | The agent answers 10 tasks per call as a JSON list; each task is still checked on its own, and a call's tokens are split evenly over its tasks | Free tier: 15 requests/minute and 500/day per model and project |
| A skill tree, ~200 skills | Per family the empty skill plus 9 candidates, each written from a different random sample of 2-4 of the empty skill's real failures on the stream tasks (prompt, expected answer, the model's answer). 50 skills, 2,000 agent-task cells, 45 proposer calls, 450 judge calls | Quota. Candidates are rewrites of the empty skill, so in the replay the proposer does not see the current incumbent |
| Judge: real pairwise comparisons | One verdict per ordered pair of a family's skills (both presentation orders); the judge sees four example stream tasks and the two notes. Repeated questions alternate the two orders | Precomputed so the replay costs nothing per policy |
| Verifier | Exact match after stripping a surrounding Markdown code fence; JSON compared in canonical form | Chat models fence answers even when told not to |
| Task suite | As section 3, with one bug fixed: half the unit conversions expected the reverse direction of what the prompt asked | Found while porting the suite |

**Pilot criteria, measured:** (A) empty-skill success 40-42.5% in all 5 families (band 25-60%): pass.
(B) every proposer output is a non-empty note, and candidates differ in value: pass, but **no candidate is worse
than the empty skill** (43 of 45 better, 2 tied), so "some candidates do not beat their parent" fails; the
conventions are learnable from one or two diffs. (C) determinism: every call is cached by its exact prompt, so a
rebuild returns identical responses; repeated live calls at temperature 0 were not measured separately.

**Replay world.** 5 families, 600 tasks per run (120 per family; each family's 30 stream tasks in a
seed-shuffled order, cycling), 50 seeds that shuffle the stream order and the order in which candidates are
offered. Live outcomes, verification outcomes (held-out tasks) and judge verdicts are all recorded answers.
Costs are charged twice: at the simulator's assumed prices and at prices **measured** from the recorded tokens
(one task rollout = 65.6 tokens; a proposal = 6.27 rollouts, a verification probe 1.19, a judge call 4.73).
