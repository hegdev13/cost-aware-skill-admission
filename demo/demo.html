Should my AI agent keep this new skill?

An AI agent keeps a short "skill" note for each kind of task. Every so often something proposes a rewrite of that note. A good rewrite helps on future tasks and a bad one hurts. Checking which kind you have costs money too. This demo runs the project's real simulator to compare seven fixed rules from the literature against a cost-aware controller that treats "find out more" as an action with a price.

1 · The race: same tasks, same candidates, different rules

Every policy sees exactly the same 1,000-task stream and the same candidate rewrites. Each line shows the extra cost a policy has paid so far compared with the cheaper of two reference points: "never change anything" and an oracle that magically knows every rewrite's true quality. Lower is better. Cost is counted in task-runs: a task costs 1, a failed task costs 5 more, and a proposal, judge call or verification run each has its own price.

2 · Inside one decision

These are real records from the controller's decision log (base prices, seed 3). At each step it states what it believes about the candidate, how much each action is expected to save, and what it chose. The hidden truth stays covered until you reveal it.

3 · Be the controller

This runs the controller's maths live in your browser. Change the prices or the evidence and watch the decision change. Its belief is a probability distribution over "how much better is the candidate than the current skill?"; every action is valued as expected cost saved minus its price.
